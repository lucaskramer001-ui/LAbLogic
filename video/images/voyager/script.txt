You'd wait two days for Voyager 1 to answer a single "hello". It's now one light-day away. So why don't we just catch up with it?

On November eighteenth, Voyager 1 became the first human-made object one light-day from Earth. That's about twenty-six billion kilometers. Even light needs a full day to get there.

It launched in nineteen seventy-seven. Our rockets are better now. Just build a faster probe. Right?

The first problem: Voyager cheated. It swung past Jupiter and Saturn and stole speed from both. New Horizons left Earth even faster, but today it's slower than Voyager.

The second problem is the head start. Voyager flies about seventeen kilometers every second, and it never stops. To catch it within twenty years, a new probe would need to fly more than three times faster.

The third problem is time. Voyager runs on a nuclear battery that loses about four watts every year. Most of its instruments are already switched off. By the time anything catches up, it will be silent.

So we don't chase Voyager. We listen, while we still can.

One day away. And a little farther every second.
