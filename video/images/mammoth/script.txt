You were promised a woolly mammoth by twenty twenty-eight. So why don't we just bring it back already?

The last mammoths died out about four thousand years ago. But their DNA survived, frozen in the ice. Edit an elephant with it, and you get a mammoth. Simple, right?

The first problem is the gap. A mammoth isn't one gene away from an elephant. The company Colossal first planned around sixty-five gene edits. Now it expects more than a hundred and fifty. So the first calf moved from twenty twenty-eight to the early twenty-thirties.

The second problem is the mother. An elephant carries a baby for about twenty-two months. And Asian elephants are endangered. Asking them to be surrogates is a big ask.

And even then, is it a mammoth? When Colossal announced "dire wolves" in twenty twenty-five, many scientists said they were gray wolves with twenty edits.

Some hope mammoth herds could protect frozen Arctic soil by trampling the snow. A small study found hints of that. But no proof at scale.

So the mammoth might come back one day. Just slower, and less real, than the headlines say.

Before we bring back the mammoth, we have to save the elephant.
