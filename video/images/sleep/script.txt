You waste about eight hours a day asleep. One teenager stayed awake for eleven days straight. So why don't we just stop?

Think about it. Eight extra hours, every single day. That's a whole second shift of life.

In 1964, a seventeen-year-old named Randy Gardner stayed awake for two hundred sixty-four hours. Scientists checked on him the whole time. His body held up, but his mind didn't. His memory slipped, he got irritable, and one observer reported hallucinations. Today, Guinness World Records won't even accept attempts at this record, because it's too dangerous.

And you don't need eleven days to feel it. After seventeen hours awake, your reactions can be about as bad as with a blood alcohol level of 0.05 percent. After twenty-four hours, it's like 0.10, above the legal driving limit in the US.

And sleep isn't just rest. In mice, scientists found that the spaces between brain cells grow by about sixty percent during sleep. Fluid rushes through and washes out waste, including a protein linked to Alzheimer's.

And when rats were kept awake completely, they were all dying within about two to five weeks.

So sleep isn't wasted time. It's when your brain takes out the trash.
