Your grandkids may never see the Titanic. Bacteria are eating it three thousand eight hundred meters down. So why don't we just pull it up?

It's the most famous shipwreck on Earth. Lift it, clean it, and put it in a museum.

The first problem is depth. The wreck lies about two and a half miles under the North Atlantic. It took until 1985 just to find it.

The second problem: there's no single ship left to lift. The Titanic broke in two as it sank. The bow and the stern lie about six hundred meters apart, and the stern was smashed on the way down.

And the steel is falling apart. Iron-eating microbes cover it in rusticles, like icicles made of rust. In 2010, scientists found a brand new species of bacteria in them, and named it after the ship. Try to lift a hull like that, and it could crumble.

The wreck is also protected. It's covered by a UNESCO convention, and a treaty between the US and the UK controls who can go inside.

More than fifteen hundred people died there. So the plan is to leave it where it is.

We can't save the ship. But we can save its story.
