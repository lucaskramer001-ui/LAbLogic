You could plant one trillion trees and still lose the climate fight. So why don't we just plant them anyway?

Trees pull carbon dioxide out of the air and lock it in wood. In twenty nineteen, a study said Earth has room for nine hundred million hectares of new forest. That's about the size of the United States. Those trees could store two hundred five billion tonnes of carbon.

Problem solved?

The first problem is the math. Other scientists said that number was too high, maybe five times too high. And we burn fossil fuels so fast, about thirty-seven billion tonnes of carbon dioxide a year, that even the big number only covers about twenty years.

The second problem is time. A seedling takes decades to become a big tree.

The third problem is the place. A lot of that "empty" land is grassland and savanna, full of wildlife. And in the far north, dark trees cover bright snow, so the ground soaks up more sunlight. There, more trees can actually warm the planet.

And forests burn. One big fire sends their carbon right back into the air.

So protect the forests we have, and plant the right trees in the right places. But the real fix is burning less.

Trees are a bonus. Not an excuse.
