You're standing on a five-thousand-degree furnace. Drill down, and you'd never pay for power again. So why don't we?

Earth's inner core is around five thousand two hundred degrees Celsius, about as hot as the surface of the Sun. And that heat isn't going anywhere. So here's the obvious fix. Drill a deep hole, pump water down, and bring up steam to spin turbines.

The problem is the drilling. The deepest hole humans have ever made is the Kola Superdeep Borehole in Russia. Soviet scientists worked on it for almost twenty years and got just over twelve kilometers down. That's about 0.2 percent of the way to Earth's center.

At the bottom, the rock was around a hundred and eighty degrees Celsius, almost twice as hot as they expected. The heat was wrecking the equipment, the money ran out, and the project stopped.

The core starts about two thousand nine hundred kilometers down. That's more than two hundred times deeper than Kola.

But here's the twist. You don't need the core. In 2009, drillers in Iceland hit magma by accident just two kilometers down, and the steam came up at over four hundred fifty degrees.

So we already tap Earth's heat where it comes close to the surface.

The heat is free. The hard part is building a drill that survives it.
