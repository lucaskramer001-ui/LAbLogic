You've been bitten by the deadliest animal on Earth. Malaria alone left about five hundred ninety-seven thousand people dead in 2023. So why don't we just wipe out every mosquito?

It sounds simple. Spray everything, drain every puddle, and the problem is gone for good.

The first catch is that there are about three thousand five hundred kinds of mosquito. Only the females bite, and most species never spread a disease to people. Of roughly four hundred thirty Anopheles species, only thirty to forty spread malaria.

The second catch is that mosquitoes are food. Their larvae feed fish and other water animals. One entomologist estimated that birds nesting in the Arctic tundra could drop by half without them. Others disagree, and nobody knows for sure.

So scientists are trying a smarter fix: target only the deadly few. In 2018, researchers at Imperial College London built a gene drive that spread through caged malaria mosquitoes until no females were born, and the cages died out. Later, in bigger cages built to mimic nature, the populations collapsed within a year.

It hasn't been tested in the wild yet.

But the plan is clear. We don't need to kill every mosquito. Just the few that kill us.
