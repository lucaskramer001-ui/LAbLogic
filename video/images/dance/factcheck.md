# Fact-check sheet - The Town That Couldn't Stop Dancing (Strasbourg, 1518)

Research 2026-11-08. britannica.com and publicdomainreview.org were blocked in the cloud session; their content
came through search snippets only. Please open the links before publishing.

| Claim (script wording) | Sources | Confidence | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| July 1518, a woman began to dance in a Strasbourg street | Wikipedia (refs Waller); Britannica; National Geographic | confirmed | named Frau Troffea in later accounts; some sources doubt she was first |
| She kept going four days, maybe six | Public Domain Review; Univ. of Illinois MPAL notes | confirmed as reported | "four to six days" |
| By August between 50 and 400 people were dancing | Wikipedia; National Geographic | disputed range | script says "somewhere between fifty and four hundred" |
| Doctors: "hot blood", cure = more dancing | Public Domain Review; Wikipedia | confirmed as reported | |
| Council cleared two guild halls and a grain market, built a wooden stage, hired musicians | National Geographic; Wikipedia | confirmed as reported | |
| Later accounts: people collapsed; some say up to 15 a day died; historians argue | Discover (citing Waller, Lancet 2009); PDR; Wikipedia ("sources that mention deaths are all later accounts") | disputed | said as disputed in the voice-over and on screen |
| Council banned public music and dancing | Wikipedia; secondary accounts (fine for performing) | confirmed as reported | |
| Dancers taken to a shrine of St Vitus; many said to recover | National Geographic (Sept 1518, "apparently cured") | confirmed as reported | shrine near Saverne |
| St Vitus believed able to curse people with dancing | Futility Closet / Waller; Wikipedia | legend (belief) | framed as belief |
| Poisoned bread (ergot) is one theory | Futility Closet; Wikipedia | theory | Waller rejects it |
| Waller: famine, disease, belief in an angry saint -> mass psychogenic illness | Waller, *A Time to Dance, a Time to Die* (2008); Lancet 373:624-625 (2009) | historian's interpretation | |
