# Topics, research and fact-checking

## Four pillars (rotate them)
| Pillar | What | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Records | strange true events (dancing plague, Dyatlov Pass, Tanganyika laughter epidemic, Mary Celeste) | facts from records; say what is disputed |
| Legends | myths and folklore people believed (St Vitus, changelings, the Pied Piper's real 1284 record) | always framed as "people believed / the legend says" |
| Experiments | real experiments on humans (Monster Study 1939, Stanford prison, Tuskegee, MKUltra, Unit 731 only without detail) | respectful, name the harm, cite apologies / settlements |
| Hoaxes | creepypastas and fakes (Russian Sleep Experiment, Slender Man, Cottingley Fairies) | tell the story, then reveal where it came from |

## Research method (from the Strategos Archive / battle-historian workflow)
1. Research 3-5 candidate topics; pick the one with a specific date + place + one strange image for the hook.
2. For every claim in the script, find **two independent sources**, preferably a historian, university, museum,
   national archive, peer-reviewed paper or a quality publication (National Geographic, Smithsonian, BBC History).
   Wikipedia only as a map to its references.
3. Fill the **fact-check sheet** (`video/images/<ep>/factcheck.md`): claim | wording in script | sources |
   confidence (confirmed / disputed / legend) | note. Numbers that vary between sources get a range or "some say".
4. Name people with their real role (Lab Logic lesson: check every person's job before the voice).
5. If a page is blocked in the cloud session (britannica.com and publicdomainreview.org were), say which facts
   came from search snippets only and ask the user to open the links before publishing.
