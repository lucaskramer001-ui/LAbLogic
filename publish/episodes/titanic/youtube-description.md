The Titanic is slowly being eaten by iron-eating microbes, about 3,800 meters down. So why don't we just raise it and put it in a museum? Because there's no single ship left to lift, the steel is falling apart, and the wreck is protected as a memorial.

The wreck lies about 2.5 miles under the North Atlantic and wasn't found until 1985. It broke in two as it sank: the bow and stern lie about 600 meters apart, and the stern was smashed on the way down. Iron-eating microbes cover the hull in rusticles, and in 2010 scientists found a new species of bacteria in them, Halomonas titanicae, named after the ship. The wreck is protected by a UNESCO convention, and a US-UK treaty controls who can enter it. More than 1,500 people died in the sinking, so the plan is to preserve it where it lies.
