Planting a trillion trees sounds like the easiest climate fix ever. So why don't we just do it? Because the math, the time and the place all work against it.

A 2019 study in Science estimated that Earth has room for about 0.9 billion hectares of new tree cover (roughly the size of the United States), which could store about 205 billion tonnes of carbon. Other scientists replied that this was overestimated, by up to about five times. Even the big number is only about 20 years of today's fossil fuel emissions (about 37 billion tonnes of CO2 in 2023; 205 billion tonnes of carbon is about 750 billion tonnes of CO2). A seedling also takes decades to grow into a big tree. Much of the "empty" land is grassland and savanna full of wildlife. In the far north, dark trees cover bright snow, so the ground absorbs more sunlight and more trees can actually warm the planet. And when forests burn, their carbon goes right back into the air.

So: protect the forests we have, plant the right trees in the right places, and burn less. Trees are a bonus, not an excuse.
