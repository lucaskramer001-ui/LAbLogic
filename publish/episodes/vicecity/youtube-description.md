GTA 6 is out, and its Vice City is based on Miami. The real Miami already floods on sunny days. So why don't we just build a giant wall around it, like the Netherlands does?

Because Miami sits on porous limestone, full of holes like Swiss cheese. At high tide, seawater pushes up through the rock from underneath, so a seawall stops the waves but the water goes under it. That's why some streets flood with no storm at all ("sunny-day flooding"). Miami's drinking water also comes from the Biscayne Aquifer right under the region, and as the sea rises, salt water pushes into it. Local planners (Southeast Florida Regional Climate Change Compact) expect sea level to be 10 to 17 inches higher by 2040 than in 2000. So Miami Beach went another way: about $400 million for pumps, valves and raised roads, with some streets raised by more than two feet.

In the game, you can just drive through the flood. In real life, Vice City has to rise with the sea.
