About 15,000 near-Earth asteroids big enough to destroy a city still haven't been found. So why don't we just nuke a dangerous asteroid like in the movies? Because blowing it to pieces can leave a cloud of rocks still heading for Earth. Scientists prefer a nudge.

Many asteroids are weak, loose piles of rubble, so a blast may scatter rather than remove the danger. In 2022, NASA's DART spacecraft hit the small asteroid Dimorphos at more than 14,000 mph and shortened its orbit by 32 minutes; NASA needed only 73 seconds to call it a success. A small push years ahead can turn a hit into a miss, and a nuclear blast stays a last resort when there's no time left. The real problem is time: only about 40% of near-Earth asteroids larger than 140 meters have been found, which is why NASA is building the NEO Surveyor space telescope.
