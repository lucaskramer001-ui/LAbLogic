Why don't we just tow icebergs to thirsty cities for drinking water? In 2018 Cape Town came weeks away from Day Zero, while Antarctica keeps breaking off giant icebergs made of fresh water.

The catch: about 90% of an iceberg hides underwater, a Woods Hole simulation says the tow to Cape Town takes about two months, and once it's parked in 15-20 °C water it could melt in weeks or even days.

In 1977 Prince Mohammed al-Faisal of Saudi Arabia backed an iceberg conference at Iowa State University and planned to tow a 100-million-tonne Antarctic iceberg to Arabia. In 2018 salvage expert Nick Sloane proposed towing one to Cape Town, estimating it could supply about a fifth to a third of the city's water for a year, at about $100 million for the tow plus tens of millions to collect the water. The city chose groundwater, desalination and saving water instead: residents cut use to about half, and Day Zero never came.
