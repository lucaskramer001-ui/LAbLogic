On November 18, 2026, Voyager 1 became the first human-made object one light-day from Earth: about 25.9 billion km (16.1 billion miles, 173 AU) away. A radio signal now needs a full day to get there, so a "hello" and its answer take about two days. It launched in 1977. Our rockets are better now, so why don't we just build a faster probe and catch up with it?

Because Voyager "cheated": it swung past Jupiter and Saturn and gained speed from both (gravity assists). New Horizons left Earth even faster, but today it's slower than Voyager. Because of the head start: Voyager flies about 17 km every second and never stops, so to catch it within 20 years a new probe would need to average more than three times its speed (about 58 km/s - our own estimate from 25.9 billion km plus 20 years of travel at 17 km/s). And because of time: Voyager's nuclear power source (RTGs) loses about 4 watts a year, most of its instruments are already switched off, and by the time anything caught up, it would be silent.

So we don't chase Voyager. We listen, while we still can. One day away, and a little farther every second.
