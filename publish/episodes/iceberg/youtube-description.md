Cape Town came close to running out of water, and Antarctica keeps shedding giant icebergs made of fresh water. So why don't we just tow an iceberg to a thirsty city?

Most of an iceberg hides underwater, the trip from Antarctica takes about two months, and once it's parked in warm water it can melt in weeks or even days.

People have floated this plan since the 1970s, and a 2023 computer study from Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution modelled towing icebergs to Cape Town. Even an iceberg that survives the trip is hard to use: without a way to slow the melting, a year of water would need one about 3 kilometres long on arrival, and nobody has a proven way to catch the meltwater before it mixes into the ocean.
