You were promised a woolly mammoth by 2028. So why don't we just bring it back already? The last mammoths died out about 4,000 years ago, but their DNA survived in the ice. Edit an elephant with it, and you get a mammoth. Simple, right?

Not quite. A mammoth isn't one gene away from an elephant: Colossal Biosciences first listed about 65 genes to edit and now expects more than 150, and in 2026 its CEO said the first mammoth-like calf is expected in the early 2030s instead of 2028. Then there's the mother: an elephant pregnancy lasts about 22 months, and Asian elephants, the closest living relatives, are endangered. And would it even be a mammoth? When Colossal announced "dire wolves" in 2025, many scientists said they were gray wolves with 20 edits in 14 genes. Some hope mammoth herds could protect Arctic permafrost by trampling the snow; a small 2022 pilot study at Pleistocene Park found hints of that, but there's no proof at scale.

So the mammoth might come back one day. Just slower, and less real, than the headlines say. Before we bring back the mammoth, we have to save the elephant.
