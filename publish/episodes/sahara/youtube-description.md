The Sahara is huge, it's the sunniest place on Earth, and almost nobody lives there. So why don't we just cover it in solar panels and power the world?

Because the power has to reach the cities. Desertec, the plan to send Sahara power to Europe, fell apart in 2013, and in 2025 the UK government turned down the Xlinks project, a roughly 4,000 km cable from Morocco. Because of sand: dust can cut a panel's output by up to about 30% in a single month, and cleaning panels usually takes water, in a desert. And because dark panels on bright sand change the climate: in a 2021 climate-model study, covering 20% of the Sahara with solar farms made the desert about 1.5 °C hotter, shifted the tropical rain belt north (drier Amazon), and warmed the whole planet by about 0.16 °C. Other models find different regional rain effects, so treat these as model results, not certainties.

So the answer isn't one giant solar farm. It's lots of smaller ones, close to the people who use them. The sun is free. Moving it isn't.
