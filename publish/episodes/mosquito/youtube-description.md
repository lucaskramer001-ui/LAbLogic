Mosquitoes are the deadliest animal on Earth: malaria alone killed about 597,000 people in 2023, according to the WHO. So why don't we just wipe out every mosquito? Because most of them aren't the problem, and scientists are working on something much more precise.

There are about 3,500 kinds of mosquito. Only the females bite, and most species never spread disease to people: of roughly 430 Anopheles species, only 30 to 40 spread malaria. Mosquitoes are also food. Their larvae feed fish, and one entomologist estimated Arctic tundra birds could drop by half without them, though others disagree. So researchers target only the deadly few. In 2018, Imperial College London showed a gene drive could spread through caged malaria mosquitoes until no females were born and the cages died out; in larger cages built to mimic nature, the populations collapsed within a year. It hasn't been tested in the wild yet.
