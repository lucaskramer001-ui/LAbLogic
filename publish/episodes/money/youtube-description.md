Why don't we just print more money and hand everyone a stack of cash? Because money isn't wealth: it's a ticket for real things like bread, and printing more tickets doesn't bake a single extra loaf.

If the money grows much faster than the stuff it can buy, prices climb instead. In Berlin a loaf of bread went from about 160 marks at the end of 1922 to around 200 billion marks by late 1923, and in November 2008 economists estimate prices in Zimbabwe were doubling about every 25 hours.
