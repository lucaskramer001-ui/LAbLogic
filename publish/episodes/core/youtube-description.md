Earth's inner core is around 5,200 °C, about as hot as the surface of the Sun. So why don't we just drill down to it for unlimited energy? Because the deepest hole humans have ever made only got about 0.2% of the way to Earth's center.

The Kola Superdeep Borehole in Russia took Soviet scientists almost 20 years and reached about 12.2 km. At the bottom the rock was around 180 °C, roughly twice what they expected; the heat wrecked the equipment, the money ran out, and drilling stopped in 1992. The core starts about 2,900 km down, more than 200 times deeper. The twist: you don't need the core. In 2009, Iceland's IDDP-1 well hit magma by accident just 2.1 km down, and its steam came up at over 450 °C. Geothermal plants already use Earth's heat where it comes close to the surface.
